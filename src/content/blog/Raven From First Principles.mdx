## Introduction

Nowadays, inference at scale is where the real money goes. Efficient models are needed to lower the cost
while ensuring good performance; and even though Transformer-based models are the de facto architecture of modern
AI models but in this blog post, rather than reaching for quantization or pruning, I'd like to go somewhere less traveled,
a hidden gem: the <Link href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13048" external>Receptance Weighted Key Value (RWKV)</Link>.

Standard softmax-based attention is the winning approach in many domains. However, there is no such thing as a free
lunch; it comes with the cost of a quadratic complexity in memory. Algorithms like _Flash-Attention_ relax the problem to make
the computation more efficient. But with with the latest versions leveraging capabilities of high end GPUs, you may be stuck
with an older version of Flash Attention if you do not have access to an H100 or B200. So on older hardware you're back to worse
memory scaling.

While softmax attention was gaining traction, another paradigm emerged: <Link href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16236" external>**Linear Attention**</Link>.
By removing the non-linearity of the softmax and rearranging the computation of attention, it turns out that one can express
transformer-based model computation as an RNN, leading us to **linear RNNs**.

Linear attention made attention more memory efficient, however it was not as competitive as softmax attention. RWKV is one of the earliest
models that made linear attention competitive at scale with a practical training recipe. As a linear RNN, its principles are rooted in the
linear attention paradigm and it elegantly blends the parallel nature of Transformer based models during training and the efficiency of RNNs at inference time.

But before getting to it, let's see how softmax-based attention can be rewritten as an RNN by breaking the non-linearity.

## From softmax attention to linear attention
